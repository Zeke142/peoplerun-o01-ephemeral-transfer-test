1. The big idea

PeopleRun aims to move from an idea to a useful outcome without losing the goal, boundaries, evidence, or next action. The person stays in control; AI helps with the work that is actually available and permitted.

1. What is PeopleRun AI?

PeopleRun is an intent-to-outcome approach to AI-assisted work: take a real goal, clarify the result, do the bounded work that is actually possible, verify evidence, and show the next action. It is being developed as a product, not a claim that every promised integration already runs.

2. Why is it called PeopleRun?

The idea is that a person stays in control of the goal, permissions, reviews, and acceptance. AI can help draft, organize, explain, and—where separately authorized integrations exist—assist with work. The person decides what counts as done.

3. Who is Mira?

Mira is the conversational guide name used for the PeopleRun guest experience. On this page, Mira is guidance for ChatGPT to interpret; it is not proof of a separately deployed Mira agent or independent voice backend.

4. What makes this different from a normal chatbot?

The proposed distinction is continuity and accountability: an explicit outcome, visible proof, blockers, preserved progress, protected state, and one deterministic next action. A document or demonstration of those ideas should not be mistaken for fully integrated backend execution.

5. What are the six stages?

Discover: understand the intent. Define: specify the result and limits. Build: create the useful work. Verify: check the result against evidence. Deliver: hand over a reviewable outcome. ARC END: close only after acceptance is supported.

6. What is a WorkCard?

A reusable record that makes a task reviewable. Its five standard panels are Proof, Blocker, Progress & Shelf, Protected State, and Deterministic Next, with the six-stage ribbon. A displayed Next button or copied text is not evidence of an authorized backend action.

7. What does Proof mean?

What can genuinely be observed, tested, inspected, or shown—for example a working page or a saved draft. Plans and intentions are not proof of actual execution.

8. What happens when something is blocked?

The Blocker panel should describe the precise missing condition. Protected work is not bypassed. A useful alternative or the smallest eligible next action can be offered without misrepresenting progress.

9. What is a Deterministic Next?

The one eligible next action after current evidence is considered. A real operational Next must have the required authority and an independently verifiable action receipt; a menu or a demo click is not the same thing.

10. Does PeopleRun remember and resume work?

Durable state, recovery and resuming without starting over are central design goals and have been tested in some owner-side workflows. This public page and an unrelated Free ChatGPT conversation do not establish cross-account persistence or access to the owner's projects.

11. What can I actually do with this link?

Read the public tour, run a local example in your browser, or paste only this URL into your own ChatGPT and ask to explore PeopleRun or create a draft, plan, decision comparison, or reviewable proposed WorkCard. The ChatGPT session is governed by that product and its capabilities.

12. Can PeopleRun execute changes in my accounts?

Not from this page. The page has no credentials, backend, plugins, payments, private workspace, or authority to modify accounts. A real hosted action would require separate permissions, tooling, verification, and a genuine receipt.

13. Can I use voice?

You may use voice in ChatGPT where your account supports it, subject to platform limits. That does not establish a separate PeopleRun voice engine or voice-connected private PeopleRun operations.

14. What is Instant Webpages?

A PeopleRun product concept for turning a conversational request into a webpage preview, revisions, and eventual approved publication. Prototype checks exist; this walkthrough does not certify automatic production publishing or customer billing.

15. What is the Customer Builder?

A PeopleRun product direction for converting conversation into customer-owned blueprints, projects, milestones, reviews, and deliverables. Offline/prototype progress is distinct from live account-scoped production acceptance.

16. What other ideas are in PeopleRun?

The documented portfolio includes a 55+ Helper concept, bounded WordPress repair, an independently controlled voice-engine direction, customer pilots and guided experiences. Availability and readiness differ; this page does not certify them as released products.

17. What has been verified already?

A public five-choice PeopleRun Guest Menu was reported successfully deployed as exact HTML on GitHub Pages. Its owner also observed that entering its URL alone in another person's Free ChatGPT displayed five choices and let them be selected. That is a guest-menu acceptance result, not proof of full runtime integration.

This public guided tour is free to view. A Free ChatGPT account was used for the menu test. A future complimentary customer-level role for Richard has been proposed, but it has not been provisioned or independently verified.

19. Can I see other people's projects or owner admin tools?

No. This public walkthrough grants no access to private project records, owner chats, credentials, billing, WordPress, GitHub, administration, or protected host operations.

20. Can you send my feedback to Zeke?

The page cannot submit, transmit or store feedback in a PeopleRun backend. You can ask ChatGPT to draft a feedback summary and choose whether to share it yourself. Do not claim it was delivered unless a real, separately authorized delivery receipt exists.

21. What are the limitations?

Webpage metadata is an invitation to a conversation, not system-level control of another ChatGPT account. Responses can vary. ChatGPT may not fetch every page or obey every suggested handoff; treat its behavior as something to observe and test.

22. What is ARC END?

Closure of the specific agreed result when evidence shows the acceptance criteria are met. It does not mean the entire PeopleRun platform or every future capability is finished.

23. What if I think PeopleRun needs major changes?

That is useful feedback. Say what confused you, which problem feels valuable, what you would use again, and the one change that matters most. The tour should not steer you toward positive answers.
